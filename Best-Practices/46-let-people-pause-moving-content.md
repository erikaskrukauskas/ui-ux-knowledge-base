# Let people pause moving content

Content that moves, blinks, scrolls, or updates by itself for more than a moment, beside other content, can be paused, stopped, or hidden. The person can also slow an auto-update.

## When it applies

Use this when the page starts the motion. Motion the person triggered is the reduced-motion rule.

## The decision

- Give a pause, stop, or hide control for automatic motion that lasts more than five seconds.
- Give a way to pause, hide, or slow automatic updates.
- A loading state that everyone must wait through can be essential. Confirm that exception. Confirm the five-second figure.

## Evidence

W3C Recommendation, WCAG 2.2, 12 December 2024: "For any moving, blinking or scrolling information that (1) starts automatically, (2) lasts more than five seconds, and (3) is presented in parallel with other content, there is a mechanism for the user to pause, stop, or hide it unless the movement, blinking, or scrolling is part of an activity where it is essential." Confirm the current page before you rely on it: [Let people pause moving content](https://www.w3.org/TR/WCAG22/#pause-stop-hide).
