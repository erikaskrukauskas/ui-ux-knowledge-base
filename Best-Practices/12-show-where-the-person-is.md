# Show where the person is and how to go back

Tell the person where they are in the task, and give them a clearly marked way back. A path they cannot place, or cannot leave, makes them guess.

## When it applies

Use this whenever a task continues across more than one view, or a view can be left.

## The decision

- Show the current place in words the person already uses for the task: the step, the section, or the object they opened.
- Keep that place visible without asking them to remember the path they took.
- Provide a direct way to go back or to leave the current interaction. Label it as leaving, not as a new commitment.
- Return them to a stable place they will recognize, with the work they already entered still there when the leave action should preserve it.

## Evidence

Nielsen Norman Group, last reviewed January 30, 2024: "You Are Here indicators on mall maps show people where they currently are, to help them understand where to go next." The same page says people need "a clearly marked emergency exit to leave the unwanted action without having to go through an extended process."
