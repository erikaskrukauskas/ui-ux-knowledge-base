# Name the outcome on the control

The words on a control tell the person what will happen next. Those words stay specific, accurate, and understandable if the surrounding sentence is skipped.

## When it applies

Use this for links, buttons, and other controls that move the person forward. Product voice and campaign lines stay in the product. See [Separate practice from product](07-separate-practice-from-product.md).

## The decision

- Name the result the person gets: the object, the action, or the place. "Save address" tells them more than "OK" or "Learn more."
- Make the result true immediately after they activate the control. A label that promises booking and opens a contact form breaks the promise.
- Write the label so it still makes sense alone. People often read the control and skip the paragraph beside it.
- Keep every word that carries that meaning, and cut the words that do not.
- Put the same meaning in the accessible name. See [Meet accessibility expectations](05-meet-accessibility-expectations.md).

## Evidence

Nielsen Norman Group: "Specific link text sets sincere expectations and fulfills them, and is substantial enough to stand alone while remaining succinct." Confirm the current article before you rely on it: [Better Link Labels: 4Ss for Encouraging Clicks](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/better-link-labels/).
