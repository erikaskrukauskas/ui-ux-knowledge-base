# Let people control a time limit

When the interface sets a time limit, the person can turn it off, adjust it, or extend it before the limit takes their work or their place in the task.

## When it applies

Use this when content will expire, refresh, advance, or log the person out on a timer the product chose. A limit that is part of a real-time event, or that would invalidate the activity if extended, is an exception named in the current criterion. Confirm that exception on the official page before you rely on it.

## The decision

- Prefer no timer. Add one only when the task requires it.
- If a timer remains, warn the person before it expires and let them extend it with a simple action, or let them turn it off or lengthen it before they meet it.
- Keep what they entered when the limit is extended.
- Do not move them to another place, or clear the task, without that warning.

## Evidence

W3C, Understanding Success Criterion 2.2.1 Timing Adjustable: "Let users turn off, adjust, or extend time limits." The goal on that page is that people have adequate time to complete tasks. Confirm the current criterion, including its exceptions, before you rely on a number or an exception: [Timing Adjustable](https://www.w3.org/WAI/WCAG22/Understanding/timing-adjustable.html).
