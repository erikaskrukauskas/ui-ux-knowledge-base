# Structure information before navigation

Decide the information architecture before the navigation. Group the tasks and objects people come to find, and name those groups in their words. Navigation is how a person reaches that structure. It is not the structure.

## When it applies

Use this when a product has more than one task or more than one group of objects, and later screens will repeat the grouping.

## The decision

- List the tasks and objects people come to find. Group them by those relationships.
- Name each group with words a person would use for the task.
- Make a first pass at that grouping before choosing navigation, so the navigation can hold the real depth and breadth.
- When a navigation pattern cannot hold the grouping people need, change the navigation.

## Evidence

Nielsen Norman Group: "IA is the information backbone of the site; navigation refers to those elements in the UI that allow users to reach specific information on the site." Confirm the current article before you rely on it: [The Difference Between Information Architecture (IA) and Navigation](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/ia-vs-navigation/).
