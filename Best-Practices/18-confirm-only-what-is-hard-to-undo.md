# Confirm only what is hard to undo

Let the person undo a recoverable action. Ask them to confirm only when the action would destroy work, spend money, or otherwise be hard to undo. A confirmation they see on every routine step gets answered without being read.

## When it applies

Use this when an action can remove, send, pay, or publish something the person may not have meant.

## The decision

- Make the action undoable when you can, and say how to undo it.
- Confirm when undo is unavailable and the consequence is serious.
- Name the object and the consequence. "Delete the Tuesday draft" is a check. "Are you sure?" is not.
- Label the choices with the two outcomes, such as "Delete draft" and "Keep draft." See [Name the outcome on the control](11-name-the-outcome-on-the-control.md).
- Do not preselect the destructive choice.
- Save an extra step, such as typing the action, for the rare case that cannot be undone and would do serious harm.

## Evidence

Nielsen Norman Group, last reviewed August 7, 2026: "Use a confirmation dialog before committing to actions with serious consequences — such as destroying users’ work or costing large amounts of money." The same article says not to use confirmation dialogs for routine actions, and to provide undo because some errors will remain. Confirm the current article before you rely on it: [Confirmation Dialogs Can Prevent User Errors — If Not Overused](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/confirmation-dialog/).
